    // The Problem

    Road maintenance today is reactive, not predictive.

    01

    Road gets damaged

    A pothole, crack, or waterlogged stretch forms — invisible to anyone until it's driven over.

    02

    Someone notices it

    Damage has to get bad enough, or dangerous enough, before a driver or pedestrian flags it.

    03

    Complaint or inspection

    The report has to reach the right department and get logged before anything moves.

    04

    Inspection team dispatched

    Someone has to physically visit the site to confirm the damage before repair is approved.

    Authorities know where a pothole is. They don't know which defects are worsening fastest, which roads should be repaired first, or what happens to traffic if a road is closed for repair.

    // How The Twin Thinks

    Five small, focused agents beat one giant model.

    VA · Vision Agent

    Detection

    A YOLO-family detector reads dashcam frames and finds potholes, cracks, waterlogging, damaged barriers, faded markings, and broken streetlights.

    SF · Sensor Fusion

    Localization

    GPS and accelerometer (IMU) readings are fused with each detection to pin it to the exact stretch of road.

    CM · Condition Model

    Scoring

    Detections are converted into a single 0–100 condition score, rule-weighted by defect type and severity.

    DF · Deterioration Forecast

    Prediction

    A trend model learns from condition history and forecasts the score forward at +7, +30, and +60 days.

    DE · Decision Engine

    Prioritization & alerting

    Condition, forecast, and traffic-risk context combine into a repair-priority rank across the whole network — then the responsible authority is alerted automatically, with a recommended repair window.

    // Differentiator

    Damage isn't risk. Context is.

    Road Risk = Damage × Exposure × Context — the same pothole scores very differently on a school-adjacent arterial road versus a quiet residential street.
